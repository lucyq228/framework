What is associated with King County sale prices?

Purpose: Describe which recorded house attributes, neighborhood context, and sale-timing patterns are associated with sale price, and flag a few patterns worth a closer look. Associations are not the effect of changing a feature.
Decision supported: Which attributes are worth briefing as linked to sale price, which mostly restate living area, and which interesting patterns need a bounded follow-up. This does not decide what to renovate or what price change to expect.
Target: price (numeric) - Recorded sale price in dollars. Higher means a higher observed price. This is an observational outcome, not a causal effect of changing a feature.
Data: 21613 rows after preparation; unit = One recorded sale (one row). The same id can appear on more than one row; those rows stay separate sales rather than a within-house panel.

Descriptive findings and observational associations do not establish causes. Statistical estimates show available n, CI, and q (Benjamini-Hochberg across recorded tests). Data-selected follow-ups remain exploratory.

Summary

Across all 21,613 sales, higher sale prices are most strongly associated with larger living area and higher construction grade, and grade remains associated after living area is held fixed. Latitude shows a price peak near 47.64 rather than a steady north-south rise, and longitude is weak as a simple rank but useful in the joint model. Waterfront sales average much higher, on only 163 sales, and that gap is not adjusted for size. Year built follows a curve with a mid-century trough. Bedroom count rises across raw bins and turns negative once living area is held fixed. Monthly means from May 2014 through May 2015 stay in a narrow band, and the monotone trend is the only test that fails false-discovery control. These are observational associations on one sale per row.

Key insights

I1. Living area and grade are the house attributes worth briefing first

On all 21,613 sales, living area and construction grade have the strongest unadjusted rank associations with sale price (Spearman 0.644 and 0.658). Living-area bin means rise from about $306,000 in the smallest tenth (n=2,200) through about $732,000, and the top tenth averages about $1,152,000 (n=2,161). Grade bins are coarser: means rise from about $295,000 (n=2,313) to about $1,258,000 (n=1,636). After living area is held fixed, the grade association shrinks to a partial Spearman of 0.369 and remains positive. In the joint linear model the standardized coefficients are about 0.420 for living area and 0.319 for grade. Permutation importance, a different quantity, ranks living area first (0.492) and grade third (0.215). The top living-area step is a higher mean regime, not a reversal. None of this is the price change from adding square feet or a grade point.

I2. Location is a peaked latitude pattern, not a straight coordinate rank

Latitude bin means on 21,613 sales rise from about $301,000 in the southernmost tenth to about $870,000 near latitude 47.64 (n about 2,160 per bin), then fall to about $480,000 in the northernmost tenth. The latitude Spearman of 0.456 summarizes that peak as if it were a steady rise. Longitude's marginal Spearman is 0.064, a negligible rank, while longitude bin means are uneven, from about $445,000 to about $666,000. Joint permutation importance puts latitude second (0.277) and longitude fourth (0.108). The joint linear coefficients are a different quantity: about 0.208 for latitude and -0.038 for longitude. Nearby living area falls from Spearman 0.572 to a partial of 0.179 after the house's own living area, so most of that context association is shared with the sold house's size. Zip code was not compared.

I3. Waterfront is a rare unadjusted gap; view keeps a modest remainder

Waterfront sales average about $1,662,000 (n=163) versus about $532,000 for the other 21,450 sales (eta 0.266). Living area is more tightly associated with price on those 163 waterfront sales (Spearman 0.850) than inland (0.641). The combined within-segment correlation is 0.642, almost the same as the pooled 0.644, because inland sales dominate the pool. The pooled living-area figure is the original screen test, not a new confirmation. View's rank association falls from 0.294 to a partial of 0.176 after waterfront and living area, so view is not only the waterfront flag, and the remainder is modest. View quantile bins collapsed to a single zero bin and are not a 0-to-4 price shape. Waterfront permutation importance is 0.013, which is not a size-adjusted dollar gap.

I4. Year built has a mid-century trough, not a newer-is-higher line

The unadjusted year-built Spearman is only 0.102 on 21,613 sales, while the joint linear coefficient is about -0.197. Those two summaries disagree, and neither is the bin shape. Quantile means fall from about $600,000 in the oldest tenth (n=2,288) to about $466,000 around 1961-1969, then rise to about $621,000 in the newest tenth (n=1,838). A quadratic check adds about 0.024 to R^2, with a turning point near 1960. Permutation importance for year built is only 0.017, so the curve is real and still a small share of joint predictive usefulness. The monotone Spearman in the curvature check and the bin profile is the same screen test. Renovation year was not part of this contrast.

I5. Bedrooms rise in the raw bins and flip only after living area is held fixed

Four bedroom bins, after duplicate edges collapse the range, have mean prices of about $396,000 (midpoint 2, n=2,972), $466,000 (n=9,824), $635,000 (n=6,882), and $798,000 (midpoint 5, n=1,935). That raw shape is monotone. The sign change is the partial Spearman, from 0.345 to -0.124, when living area is held fixed on the same 21,613 sales. The raw bedroom correlation is one test shared by the screen, the adjustment, and the bin profile. Joint permutation importance for bedrooms is about 0, and the joint linear coefficient is about -0.087, which agrees in sign with the partial rank and not with the raw bins. The partial does not hold location or grade fixed, and it is not the price change from adding a bedroom.

I6. This 13-month window does not support a sale-price trend

Monthly mean prices from 2014-05 through 2015-05 stay about $508,000 to $562,000. The low is February 2015 (about $508,000, n=1,250) and the high is April 2015 (about $562,000, n=2,231). May 2015 has 646 sales because the file ends on 2015-05-27. The monotone trend on 13 periods is r=-0.0165, and the interval runs from about -0.56 to 0.54. This is the only one of 38 unique tests that fails Benjamini-Hochberg control at 0.05 (q=0.957). The other 37 pass. A null trend does not mean every month matches; it means there is no monotone path worth briefing in this short window, and the window cannot separate a seasonal cycle from a one-time shift.

Caveat: The trend correlation uses 13 period means, not 21,613 independent month effects.

Caveat: May 2015 is a partial month.

Caveat: Failing to find a line does not by itself describe within-month spikes. The observed monthly band is the descriptive check, and it is narrow beside the sale-price spread.

Next step: Do not brief a 2014-2015 price trend from this file. A seasonal claim needs a longer calendar in a new scope.

I7. Bathrooms, floors, condition, and lot size are not separate briefing items

No adjusted rank correlation or bin profile was run for bathrooms, floors, condition, lot size, or nearby lot size. The screen already marks condition (Spearman 0.018), lot size (0.075), and nearby lot size (0.063) as negligible, and the overlap matrix flags the two lot columns as one signal (0.922). Bathrooms rank at 0.497, and the matrix ties them to living area (0.746) and grade (0.658), just under the 0.8 one-signal flag. Floors rank at 0.322 and overlap grade (0.502) and year built (0.552). In the joint linear model the standardized coefficients are about 0.081 for bathrooms, 0.019 for floors, 0.053 for condition, 0.016 for lot size, and -0.029 for nearby lot size, beside 0.420 for living area. Permutation importance for these five is about 0 to 0.003, which is a different quantity and was not treated as the redundancy test. They are omitted from the briefing list because the screen and the matrix already place them with size, grade, vintage, or each other.

Limitations

  • Population is all 21,613 sales, one row per sale. There are 21,436 distinct ids, so repeat sales are too rare for a within-house comparison. Extreme prices, lots, and bedroom counts were kept. Price skew is 4.02, the median is $450,000, and the mean is about $540,088.
  • Results are observational associations. Overlapping size, grade, and location can share a signal. No result estimates the price change from renovating, adding a bedroom, raising grade, or gaining waterfront.
  • View and yr_renovated quantile bins each collapsed to one bin of all 21,613 rows at 0. A renovated-versus-not contrast and a view-score ladder were not tested. The renovation adjustment in H9 was not run for that reason.
  • Waterfront cannot be the focal column of an adjusted correlation. The mean gap is unadjusted for living area. Joint importance is not a substitute.
  • Bathrooms were not given a partial correlation after living area. The decision that they restate size and grade uses the overlap matrix (0.746 and 0.658) and the screen, not an adjusted rank.
  • Zip code, above-grade area, and basement area were excluded from the predictors. Latitude and longitude are not a neighborhood ranking.
  • Rank correlations, partial correlations, linear coefficients, and permutation importance are different quantities. Shared test_id values are one test. Data-selected follow-ups (year built, waterfront segments, view adjustment, bedroom bins) remain exploratory. Thirty-seven of 38 unique tests pass Benjamini-Hochberg at 0.05; the monthly trend does not (q=0.957).
